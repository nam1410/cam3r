01Abstract

Recovering dense 3D geometry from unposed images remains a foundational challenge in computer vision. Current state-of-the-art models are predominantly trained on perspective datasets, which implicitly constrains them to a standard pinhole camera geometry. As a result, these models suffer from significant geometric degradation when applied to wide-angle imagery captured via non-rectilinear optics, such as fisheye or panoramic sensors. To address this, we present CAM3R, a Camera-Agnostic, feed-forward Model for 3D Reconstruction capable of processing images from wide-angle camera models without prior calibration. Our framework consists of a two-view network which is bifurcated into a Ray Module (RM) to estimate per-pixel ray directions and a Cross-view Module (CVM) to infer radial distance with confidence maps, pointmaps, and relative poses. To unify these pairwise predictions into a consistent 3D scene, we introduce a Ray-Aware Global Alignment framework for pose refinement and scale optimization while strictly preserving the predicted local geometry. Extensive experiments on various camera model datasets, including panorama, fisheye and pinhole imagery, demonstrate that CAM3R establishes a new state-of-the-art in pose estimation and reconstruction.

02Method

Two modules, one feed-forward pass.

The Ray Module answers what each camera sees. A geometric encoder produces class tokens, two transformer ray-encoder layers regress spherical-harmonic expansion coefficients, and an inverse SH transform reconstructs a continuous per-pixel field of unit ray directions. Because the field is expressed in a continuous basis rather than through a parametric lens model, one set of weights covers equirectangular panoramas, fisheye captures and pinhole photographs alike. The module is shared across both views.

The Cross-view Module answers where the geometry is and where the other camera stands. A Siamese encoder extracts features from both views and a dual transformer decoder alternates self-attention and cross-attention so each branch is conditioned on the other. DPT heads regress a per-pixel radial distance together with a confidence map, and a relative pose head estimates the rigid transform from the second view into the first. Radial distance is used instead of Z-depth because Z-depth is ill-defined once the field of view approaches a hemisphere.

A local pointmap is then simply the product of the two: the ray direction scaled by its radial distance. Lens geometry and scene geometry stay factorised rather than entangled in a single Cartesian regression, and mapping the second view through the predicted pose yields the aligned two-view reconstruction shown below.

Both clouds are the fused two-view overlay: view 0 in its own camera frame, plus view 1 mapped into that frame by the predicted relative pose. They are raw network outputs with no global alignment or post-processing, subsampled to 120k points for the web. CAM3R recovers geometry up to a global scale, so each cloud is recentred on its bounding sphere and normalised to unit radius before display; that puts prediction and ground truth in a common frame and makes the shape comparison the meaningful one. The .ply files written by the evaluation script keep their original coordinates.

05Multi-view reconstruction

Ray-Aware Global Alignment fuses the pairwise predictions above into a single globally consistent scene by optimising poses and per-image scales with the ray directions frozen, so points may only slide along their own rays.

Note. The global alignment code is not part of the current release; this repository provides the two-view network it is built on.
